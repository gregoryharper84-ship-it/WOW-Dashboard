Yes. Linemaker’s breakdown is doing something your current run did not do well enough: it turns **news, roster changes, matchup-specific stats, recent form, pitch characteristics and price thresholds into one coherent game thesis**.

The WOW framework already names several of these requirements—opponent context, game scripts, market comparison, failure paths and lineup/status checks—but the live backend currently lacks enough structured inputs to execute them consistently.  The control-plane specification also requires a standardized evidence packet before probability publication, rather than loosely attaching news after the estimate. 

## What should be added

### 1. Post-deadline roster-strength engine

This is the biggest missing component.

The model should stop using season record and full-season team statistics as though the current roster were unchanged. Add a **roster delta snapshot** that compares the team before and after the trade deadline.

Required fields:

```text
roster_snapshot_timestamp
players_added
players_removed
injured_players_unavailable
projected_lineup_war_added
projected_lineup_war_lost
starter_quality_delta
bullpen_quality_delta
bench_depth_delta
defensive_quality_delta
baserunning_delta
roster_continuity_penalty
minor_league_replacement_count
```

Then create:

```text
current_roster_strength
=
season_baseline_strength
+ acquisition_adjustment
- departure_adjustment
- injury_adjustment
- replacement_uncertainty
```

For the Mets example, the model should explicitly ask:

* Which pitchers were actually traded?
* Which relievers are available tonight?
* Which hitters are missing?
* Who replaced the departed players?
* How large is the uncertainty from recent call-ups?
* Are full-season Mets statistics still representative?

This should be a required gate for games played immediately after the trade deadline.

```text
POST_DEADLINE_ROSTER_STATE_UNRESOLVED
=> no calibrated game probability
=> maximum MODEL_QUALIFIED_HOLD
```

### 2. Confirmed-lineup impact module

Linemaker’s analysis makes the lineup itself central. Your model needs a structured comparison between the projected lineup and the team’s normal lineup.

Add:

```text
confirmed_lineup
missing_regulars
replacement_hitters
lineup_woba_vs_pitcher_hand
lineup_k_rate_vs_pitcher_hand
lineup_bb_rate_vs_pitcher_hand
lineup_iso_vs_pitcher_hand
lineup_contact_rate
lineup_chase_rate
lineup_speed_score
lineup_defensive_value
lineup_strength_vs_season_average
```

The important improvement is to calculate statistics for **tonight’s nine hitters**, not just “Mets offense” or “Guardians offense.”

For example:

```text
projected_lineup_quality_vs_LHP
projected_lineup_quality_vs_RHP
```

That would let the model properly evaluate a game with two left-handed starters.

### 3. Pitch-quality and pitch-mix trend engine

The Linemaker breakdown discusses velocity, whiff rate, curveball usage and pitch-specific performance. WOW needs these as structured model inputs rather than prose.

For each starter:

```text
fastball_velocity
velocity_change_last_3_starts
pitch_mix_season
pitch_mix_last_3_starts
pitch_usage_change
whiff_rate_by_pitch
csw_rate_by_pitch

hard_hit_rate
barrel_rate
first_pitch_strike_rate
zone_rate
walk_rate
pitches_per_plate_appearance
times_through_order_penalty
```

Then add a pitch-mix matchup score:

```text
pitch_mix_matchup_score
=
Σ (
pitch_usage
× pitcher_pitch_quality
× opponent_vulnerability_to_pitch
)
```

A claim such as “Cantillo increased curveball usage to 50%” should only affect the probability when:

1. It is verified from pitch-tracking data.
2. The sample dates are recorded.
3. The opposing lineup’s performance against that pitch is measured.
4. The change is large enough to survive regression toward the season baseline.

WOW’s baseball rules already require pitch mix, opponent profile, workload, weather and bullpen analysis; this needs to become an actual input contract rather than a checklist. 

### 4. Starting-pitcher pathway model

Do not compare starters using ERA alone.

The game model should estimate:

```text
starter_expected_innings
starter_run_distribution
starter_strikeout_distribution
starter_walk_distribution
starter_baserunner_distribution
starter_home_run_distribution
probability_exit_before_4_IP
probability_exit_before_5_IP

probability_allow_0_to_2_runs
probability_allow_3_plus_runs
```

It should also separate:

```text
P(team wins | normal starter outing)
P(team wins | early starter failure)
P(team wins | starter dominates)
```

That is the moneyline version of the WOW pitcher failure-path model, which already emphasizes that normal-outing performance cannot be treated as unconditional. 

### 5. Bullpen availability—not just bullpen ERA

Add a daily bullpen ledger:

This is particularly important for a recently dismantled or reconstructed bullpen. The model should distinguish:

* season bullpen ERA;
* current active bullpen talent;
* relievers actually available tonight;
* performance after a starter exits early.

Scoring 11 runs in six games should not automatically trigger a large downgrade if expected metrics were much better. Conversely, weak expected contact would support a real downgrade.

### 11. Explicit game-script simulation

This is the largest reasoning improvement.

Simulate at least these branches:


### 14. Probability bridge

The model needs to show **how it moved from the market prior to the final estimate**.

Recommended format:

```text
No-vig market prior                     41.7%

Post-deadline roster adjustment         -1.4%

```

The actual numbers must come from the model—not be manually assigned—but this presentation would make the reasoning auditable.

## What should **not** be copied from Linemaker

The writing style is useful, but several practices require stronger controls.

### Do not trust unsourced factual claims

The supplied breakdown contains specific claims about:

* trades;
* injuries;
* pitch usage;
* whiff rates;
* bullpen ranking;
* recent offense;
* lineup absences;
* opponent records against left-handed starters.

Those need independent verification before entering the model. The text itself explicitly says it lacks live odds and real-time projections, so its probability and price conclusions should be treated as research hypotheses, not model outputs.

### Do not combine team ERA and bullpen ERA

A figure such as 3.76 must be identified precisely:

```text
team ERA
starter ERA
bullpen ERA
available bullpen projected ERA
```

Those are not interchangeable.

### Do not double-count market information

If roster sales, injuries and lineup weakness already caused the Mets price to move from +120 to +140, the model must not apply the full news penalty again without checking what the market has absorbed.

Add:

```text
market_news_absorption_score
```

### Do not infer a player-prop edge from the game thesis

“Cantillo is favored” does not prove Over 5.5 strikeouts. That prop requires:

* expected batters faced;
* expected innings;
* Mets lineup strikeout rate;
* umpire;
* pitch count and leash;
* exact Over and Under prices;
* push treatment at whole-number lines;
* failure paths.

## Bottom line

What your model is missing is not simply “more statistics.” It needs a **current-state MLB moneyline model** that converts:

```text
trade-deadline changes
+ confirmed lineup
+ starter pitch quality
+ platoon matchup
+ available bullpen
+ current offense
+ opponent-adjusted strength
+ weather
+ game scripts
+ current market information
```

into one calibrated distribution.

Linemaker’s biggest strength is **narrative synthesis**. WOW should add that synthesis layer, but probabilities and labels must still come only from verified structured inputs and the backend. The narrative should explain the model—not become the model.
