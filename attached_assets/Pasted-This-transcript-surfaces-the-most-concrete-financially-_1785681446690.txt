## Biggest new finding: this isn't actually a parlay

Look at what got built: Polymarket (Valkyries ML) + Polymarket (Wings ML) + HardRock (Arike prop) + DraftKings (Harrison prop). Four legs, three different platforms, and the tool says outright: *"The combined parlay price isn't in my feed, so I won't invent a number."*

That's not a caveat — that's a confession that **no such parlay exists**. You cannot combine legs across separate books/exchanges into a single payout structure. Polymarket doesn't know about your DraftKings leg. There is no shared slip, no combined odds, no single stake-to-payout ratio. What Linemaker actually built is **four independent single bets**, marketed with language like "Full Four-Leg Ticket" and "your best parlay from this list" that implies one combined wager.

This is a real-money risk, not just an analytical nitpick — someone could place all four legs believing they've bought one parlay ticket with parlay-level payout odds, when they've actually placed four flat bets with four separate stakes and four separate outcomes. Nothing in the first two audits caught this because those focused on single-market accuracy, not slip *construction*. This one is structural.

## Second finding: exactly the correlation pattern your spec already bans

Section 27.1 caps props per game and requires "intentional and approved correlation." Section 28.3 says "limit correlated game scripts." wow-correlation-guard exists specifically to catch this. Here's what happened anyway:

The tool explicitly frames this as diversification — *"you now have one leg from each game"* — but pairing a team's ML with a player prop **from that same team's same game**, where the prop's own stated rationale depends on the ML's outcome, is same-game stacking by definition, not diversification. If either game turns out closer than expected, both legs in that pair are exposed to the same risk simultaneously. This is precisely the "heavy-favorite player scoring props" trap pattern your spec's failure-tag library already names.
