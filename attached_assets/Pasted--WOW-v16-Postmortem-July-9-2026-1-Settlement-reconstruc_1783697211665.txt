# 2. What actually went wrong

## Failure 1 — A good selection pool was converted into a bad five-leg product

The underlying side selection went:

* Kalshi combo: 3–2
* Two duplicated Phillies singles: 2–0
* Mariners single: settlement conflict

The most damaging mistake was not simply picking Seattle and Phoenix incorrectly. It was requiring **all five independent selections to win simultaneously**.

Even when every leg has a seemingly strong 65% probability:

[
0.65^5 \approx 11.6%
]

At 70% per leg:

[
0.70^5 \approx 16.8%
]

Thus, a five-team combo can lose most of the time even when every component is individually better than a coin flip. Yesterday transformed a decent 3–2 card into a complete loss.

### Verdict

**Primary failure: execution structure, not total model collapse.**

The near-perfect prior days likely hid this risk because favorable variance allowed several correlated execution mistakes to survive without punishment.

---

## Failure 2 — Seattle ignored a strong opponent-state signal

Miami entered the finale having:

* Won the first two games of the series.
* Shut Seattle out 2–0 the previous night.
* Produced the best MLB record since June 1 at 25–8.
* Reached five consecutive wins before winning the finale.
* Received a returning starter while Seattle was exposed to series-sweep and road-form risk. ([Reuters][3])

The Seattle side appears to have been selected primarily from baseline team or starting-pitcher strength while underweighting:

* Miami’s current form.
* Miami’s home performance.
* Seattle’s failure to score in Game 2.
* Sweep dynamics.
* Defensive execution risk.
* Whether the market price already incorporated Bryce Miller’s strong season numbers.

Bryce Miller’s pregame profile may have looked attractive, but he allowed nine hits, four walks and six runs in five innings. This is not evidence that pitcher metrics are useless; it shows that the price and opposing-team context were not sufficiently stress-tested. ([Reuters][1])

### Correct retrospective label

**Seattle: LLP_REJECT_SERIES_STATE_AND_PRICE**, or at best **LLP_WATCH**.

It should not have been eligible for both a single-entry exposure and a five-market combo without a stronger independent consensus edge.

## Failure 3 — Phoenix likely passed on team reputation rather than verified game-specific edge

The Phoenix selection lost while Atlanta won. Unlike the clean Milwaukee and Philadelphia outcomes, the screenshots provide no evidence that Phoenix had:

* A fresh consensus no-vig advantage.
* A confirmed injury/rotation edge.
* A model probability exceeding market breakeven.
* A market move in Phoenix’s direction.
* A strong enough adjusted edge to survive the combo correlation and multiplicative payout tax.

This is exactly what the current LLP rules are designed to prevent: **a probable team is not automatically a mispriced team**.

### Correct retrospective label

**Phoenix: EDGE_UNPROVEN / LLP_WATCH**, unless the archived recommendation row can show fresh odds, no-vig consensus, model probability and adjusted edge.

A result alone cannot establish that Phoenix was a bad prediction. The process problem is that Phoenix was allowed into a five-market product without visible proof that its probability exceeded its purchase price.

## Bottom-line diagnosis

**The model was not suddenly broken yesterday.**

The card produced three correct and two incorrect event calls. What broke was the conversion layer:

1. Five selections were bundled into an extremely fragile product.
2. Duplicate exposure was concentrated on Philadelphia.
3. Seattle was exposed across multiple products.
4. Settlement records were not reconciled before grading.
5. A 3–2 event card became a total Kalshi loss.

This was an **execution-governance failure with one meaningful matchup-evaluation leak: Seattle**.

# 4. Required WOW v16 patch

## WOW-PATCH-2026-07-10-CROSS-PLATFORM-COMBO-AND-SETTLEMENT-GOVERNANCE

**Purpose:** Prevent individually reasonable winner selections from being converted into negative-EV multi-market combinations, stop duplicate events from inflating model performance, and reconcile contradictory settlements across Kalshi and PrizePicks.

### Rule A — Combo size restriction

During Reliability Freeze:

```text
Kalshi sports combo maximum: 2 markets
3+ market sports combos: REJECT_BAD_STRUCTURE
4–5 market combos: HARD_REJECT_COMBO_MULTIPLICATION
```

No exception based solely on individual win probability.

A two-market combo may only reach dry-run playable status when:

* Both legs independently qualify.
* Fresh direct orderbook data exists.
* Both clear their individual edge floors after execution friction.
* Joint probability and offered combo payout produce positive EV.
* There is no duplicated event exposure elsewhere in the session.

### Rule B — Joint-EV calculation required

For every combination:

```python
joint_model_probability = product(adjusted_leg_probabilities)
combo_breakeven_probability = combo_cost / combo_max_return
combo_edge = joint_model_probability - combo_breakeven_probability
```

If dependence exists, apply correlation adjustment rather than multiplying raw probabilities.

Missing combo payout or missing adjusted leg probability:

```text
COMBO_EV_UNOBTAINABLE
REJECT_BAD_STRUCTURE
```

### Rule C — One analytical result per event

Multiple entries on the same side of the same event:

```text
financial_entries += N
model_observation_count = 1
calibration_observation_count = 1
```

Duplicates cannot increase:

* Model win count.
* Accuracy percentage.
* Calibration sample size.
* Archetype graduation evidence.

### Rule D — Cross-platform event normalization

Normalize all entries using:

```text
sport
league
event_date
away_team
home_team
selected_side
market_type
```

Examples:

```text
Seattle = Mariners = SEA
Miami = Marlins = MIA
Philadelphia = Phillies = PHI
```

Before final settlement, aggregate PrizePicks, Kalshi and sportsbook entries under one canonical `event_id`.

### Rule E — Contradictory settlement gate

When platforms disagree:

```text
final_label = SETTLEMENT_SOURCE_CONFLICT
bankroll_status = PENDING_RECONCILIATION
model_result = DO_NOT_CREDIT
calibration_eligible = false
```

Resolution priority:

1. Official league result.
2. Platform transaction/bankroll credit.
3. Platform settlement display.
4. Screenshot interpretation.

The green “Win” label alone cannot override the official event result.

### Rule F — Series-state audit for MLB winner markets

For Games 2–4 of an MLB series, require:

* Current series score.
* Previous-game run production.
* Opponent streak.
* Home/away recent performance.
* Bullpen workload.
* Starter change/return context.
* Whether the current market price has already absorbed starting-pitcher strength.

Trigger:

```text
Opponent won first two games
OR opponent streak >= 5
OR selected team scored <= 1 run in previous game
```

Then:

```text
SERIES_STATE_CAUTION
```

Two or more triggers cap the selection at `LLP_WATCH` unless sportsbook consensus and model probability still clear the edge floor after an additional uncertainty tax.

### Rule G — No streak-based confidence upgrade

Previous near-perfect days cannot raise today’s playability tier.

```text
recent_win_streak is reporting metadata only
recent_win_streak cannot alter model_probability
recent_win_streak cannot loosen edge_floor
recent_win_streak cannot increase combo_size
recent_win_streak cannot increase stake
```
