My take: the framework has useful ideas, but it is too loose for WOW/LLP approval. I would use it as a scouting memo, not as an engine rule.

What is good

The best part is the sports-moneyline concept:

Compare sportsbook no-vig fair probability vs Kalshi executable YES/NO price.

That is exactly what your LLP-Kalshi bridge should do. Kalshi markets are binary contracts where prices represent market-implied probability, and the orderbook returns YES bids and NO bids rather than normal sportsbook odds.  

The second useful idea is line lag. For WNBA/MLB, the best Kalshi opportunities would likely come from delayed reaction to:

confirmed lineups
pitcher scratches
late injury/status changes
weather/roof changes
starting lineup confirmations
bullpen availability

That fits your current WNBA/MLB focus.

What is dangerous or incomplete

1. The moneyline conversion table is too simple

The table is directionally right, but it uses raw implied probability, not no-vig fair probability.

Example:

-150 raw implied probability = 150 / (150 + 100) = 60%

But if the other side is +130:

+130 raw implied probability = 100 / (130 + 100) = 43.48%
Total raw book probability = 103.48%
No-vig favorite probability = 60 / 103.48 = 57.98%

So comparing Kalshi 52¢ against “60%” overstates the edge. The engine must compare Kalshi price against consensus no-vig, not one book’s raw ML.

2. It ignores executable price

The example says “buy at 52¢,” but the engine must verify that 52¢ is actually executable. Kalshi orderbooks show active YES and NO bids only; a YES ask has to be derived from the NO bid side, because a YES bid at X¢ is equivalent to a NO ask at 100-X¢.  

So the rule should be:

Use YES ask / executable price, not midpoint or last traded price.

3. It ignores fees and spread

Kalshi charges trading fees, and some markets can have different fee treatment.  

So “52¢ vs 58% fair” is not automatically playable. Your bridge needs:

edge after spread
edge after fee/friction
edge after liquidity check
edge after settlement audit
edge after source freshness

4. The AI-market section is not useful for your current goal

The AI-market section is mostly narrative trading. It may be interesting, but it does not help your immediate WNBA/MLB edge build. Also, those examples require current, source-verified market and news validation every time. I would not let AI/tech contracts into your money engine right now.

Label it:

RESEARCH_INTEREST only

5. The “quant bot” section conflicts with your current safety rule

Building a model is fine. Automated execution is not.

For your system:

Allowed: scanner, fair-price model, alert, dry-run ledger, calibration.
Not allowed: live auto-trading, order placement, market orders, can_execute=true.

How I would convert this into a WOW/LLP rule

Use this cleaned-up version:

Kalshi Sports ML Edge Rule — WNBA/MLB Only
A Kalshi sports winner candidate may be evaluated only when:
1. Real MLB/WNBA winner-market inventory exists.
2. Market maps exactly to a game/team.
3. Settlement rule is captured and unambiguous.
4. Orderbook is fresh Grade A/B.
5. Executable YES/NO price is available.
6. Sportsbook consensus no-vig fair probability is available.
7. Model probability is available from LLP.
8. Edge is calculated after:
   - no-vig normalization
   - Kalshi executable price
   - spread
   - fees/friction
   - shrinkage
   - liquidity
9. Minimum post-friction edge is ≥2.5%.
10. dry_run_only=true and can_execute=false always.
No midpoint approvals.
No raw-moneyline approvals.
No single-book approvals.
No AI/tech market bleed into WNBA/MLB moneyline logic.

My verdict

Useful concept, bad execution if taken literally.

For your system, keep only the sports ML part and rewrite it into this:

Find WNBA/MLB Kalshi winner markets where:
LLP true probability > Kalshi executable probability + friction by at least 2.5%,
with fresh orderbook, exact mapping, clean settlement rules, and no execution.

That is the sharp version.
