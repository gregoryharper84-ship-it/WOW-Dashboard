Good news first: none of these are structurally as hard as they might look, but they land in a clear order — NFL is the easy one, Tennis is medium with a real ceiling, Golf is the biggest rebuild.

**NFL — smallest lift.** Most of the scaffolding already exists: `wow-gate-enforcer` and `wow-market-sources` already list NFL as a supported sport for gates and consensus odds, and ESPN (already integrated for injury/starter status) covers NFL rosters and schedules too. The missing piece is a game-log fetch module in the same shape as the NBA/MLB ones from Task #76 — `nfl-data-py` is a free, actively maintained Python package sourced from nflfastR/Pro-Football-Reference that returns exactly this kind of per-game player data. Counting-stat props (passing/rushing/receiving yards, receptions) reuse the same Poisson model already coded for NBA/WNBA — it's the same math, different sport routing. The one new judgment call is anytime-TD-style binary props, which `wow-gate-enforcer` already flags as a single-event prop type requiring justification, so the gate logic doesn't need new rules, just NFL wired into it. This is close to "plumbing," not new architecture.

**Tennis — medium lift, with a ceiling worth naming up front.** There's a free, MIT-licensed historical match database (TennisMyLife) covering ATP results and WTA since 1990 in CSV form, which could seed an L10-style game log with a refresh pipeline. But it's a downloadable dataset, not a live feed, so it needs real automation work to stay current in-season. The bigger issue is the exact failure mode you just saw: coverage is much better for tour-level ATP/WTA than for ITF/Challenger-level matches, and PrizePicks slates lean heavily on that lower tier — the players in your screenshot are a good example. So even after building this, expect the model to keep failing closed on a meaningful chunk of tennis slates, not because the engineering is wrong but because the data genuinely doesn't exist for those players. On top of the data work, "Fantasy Score" itself needs to be reverse-engineered as a weighted composite (games won, sets, aces, etc.) and modeled as a sum of several distributions, not a single Poisson draw — closer in shape to the combo-prop handling already built than to something new, but still real design work.

**Golf — biggest lift.** Nothing about the current pipeline maps cleanly onto golf. There's no L5/L10 "games" — it's per-round, per-tournament, with head-to-head matchup props (lower score wins) needing a pairwise/Elo-style model instead of Poisson, and total-strokes/fantasy-point props needing a Gaussian model around expected score instead of a count model. Data Golf has the right underlying data — round-level scoring and strokes-gained across 22 tours — but its free tier looks limited from what's publicly documented; full access likely means a paid subscription, which is a business decision, not an engineering one. Gate logic also needs new concepts entirely: "lineup confirmed" doesn't exist in golf, the equivalent risks are withdrawal and missed-cut, which change what "availability" even means.

If you want to sequence these: NFL first (fast, reuses everything), then decide whether Tennis is worth building given its coverage ceiling, and treat Golf as its own project with a data-budget decision up front rather than something to slot in alongside the others.
