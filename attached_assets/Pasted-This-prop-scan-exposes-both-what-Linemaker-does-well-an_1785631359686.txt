This prop scan exposes both **what Linemaker does well** and **where WOW can outperform it decisively**.

## What Linemaker’s prop logic appears to be

Its ranking formula is roughly:

```text
recent hit rates
+ season hit rate
+ average versus line
+ simple matchup/park/defense rank
+ injury/usage narrative
= “highest hit probability”
```

The strongest signals in its output are:

* L5/L10/L15/L20 agreement
* Season hit rate
* Mean above the line
* Low thresholds such as 0.5 hits
* Basic defense-versus-position rank
* Recent role or usage
* Price shown for context

That is a useful **first-pass discovery engine**, but it is not a complete probability model.

## What it does well

### 1. Broad cross-sport scanning

It pulls candidates from:

* MLB batter props
* WNBA points, assists and threes
* Multiple sportsbooks
* Fantasy platforms
* Matchup and injury searches

This is exactly the kind of broad discovery your universal parallel router should perform. Discovery should raise research priority only, not approve the prop. 

### 2. Multiple historical windows

Using L10, L20 and season together is better than relying on one streak.

This helps separate:

* short-term form;
* medium-term persistence;
* full-season baseline.

WOW should add **L15 and L20 as optional stability windows**, while keeping role-matched L5/L10 as the main recent ledger.

### 3. Low-threshold candidate discovery

Linemaker correctly recognizes that:

* 0.5 hits;
* modest points thresholds;
* modest assist thresholds;
* low three-pointer thresholds

can produce high raw hit rates.

That is valuable for candidate generation.

### 4. Easy-to-read explanations

The write-ups make each play understandable:

* average versus line;
* hit-rate windows;
* road/home splits;
* matchup;
* main risk.

The presentation is worth borrowing.

# Major logic flaws

## 1. It mixed “remaining today” with tomorrow’s slate

The request was for props remaining **today**, but it included:

* Indiana at Minnesota on Sunday;
* Los Angeles at Portland on Sunday.

Those are next-day events, not remaining-today events.

That is a direct slate-integrity failure. The universal Slate Integrity Expert explicitly requires tomorrow’s events to be excluded from today’s slate. 

This should have returned:

```text
WRONG_DATE
REMOVE_FROM_TODAY_POOL
```

## 2. It ranks historical hit rate as probability

The model notes explicitly say:

> historical hit rates are how the model ranks probability

That is the core weakness.

Historical hit rate is not the same as predictive probability because it ignores:

* opponent quality;
* lineup position;
* projected minutes;
* starter matchup;
* usage changes;
* injuries;
* sample size;
* distribution variance;
* regression;
* exact price and settlement.

WOW already prohibits approval from L5/L10 alone and requires calibrated lower bounds. 

## 3. It does not show a real probability

The output gives hit rates, but it does not publish:

```text
raw model probability
calibrated point probability
calibrated lower bound
confidence interval
model method
```

Therefore, “highest hit probability” is really:

> highest observed historical hit-rate profile.

That is not the same thing.

## 4. It ignores uncertainty and variance

Example:

### Kelsey Mitchell over 2.5 threes

It cites:

* 10/10 recent;
* 14/15;
* season average 2.83.

But a mean of 2.83 on a line of 2.5 is not a large distributional margin. The prop requires at least three makes, and three-point makes are high variance.

A proper model needs:

```text
attempt distribution
minutes distribution
three-point percentage distribution
opponent shot-profile allowance
teammate availability
game script
P(3+ makes)
```

A recent streak does not guarantee a strong lower bound.

## 5. It uses “law of averages” incorrectly

The Mitchell analysis says:

> if she gets her usual volume, the law of averages is heavily on the Over.

That is misleading. The law of averages does not imply that a recent streak makes the next Over more likely. It could just as easily invite regression.

WOW should explicitly ban:

```text
LAW_OF_AVERAGES_SUPPORT
HOT_STREAK_AS_PROBABILITY
```

## 6. It treats average margin as safety

Kelsey Plum points:

* average near 24;
* line 14.5.

That looks strong, but the output does not verify:

* expected minutes;
* current role;
* opponent;
* blowout risk;
* injury status;
* whether the event is actually today;
* whether the line is stale or promotional.

A large mean-to-line gap is useful, but not sufficient.

## 7. It recommends same-player companion legs

It says Plum assists is a “natural companion leg” to Plum points.

That is poor slip governance.

Same-player props share:

* minutes;
* injury risk;
* foul trouble;
* blowout risk;
* possession volume;
* role changes.

The current optimizer requires duplicate exposure and correlation controls, weakest-leg removal, and smaller-card preference. 

These two props should be treated as a **shared-thesis cluster**, not a natural pairing.

## 8. It encourages a same-game Dodgers stack

It offers:

> stack the Dodgers trio into a same-game parlay

Freeman, Pages and opposing-side Rafaela hit props share:

* game environment;
* pitcher performance;
* weather;
* umpire;
* game length;
* bullpen quality;
* lineup volume.

Even if each leg is individually plausible, the combined structure requires dependence modeling. Your current slip optimizer prohibits relying on independent multiplication for correlated legs. 

## 9. It overuses batter hit streaks

For Freeman, Rafaela and Pages, the primary evidence is:

* recent hit streak;
* season hit rate;
* park factor.

Missing:

* confirmed batting order;
* opposing starter handedness;
* projected plate appearances;
* platoon split;
* contact quality;
* strikeout rate;
* bullpen matchup;
* pinch-hit risk;
* injury;
* exact settlement rules.

For 0.5 hits, the key model should estimate:

```text
P(at least one hit)
=
1 - P(no hit across projected plate appearances)
```

That requires a plate-appearance distribution and per-PA hit probability, not merely game hit rate.

## 10. BvP and opponent claims are weakly handled

It says:

> faced the Dodgers once and went Over easily

A one-game sample is not meaningful predictive evidence.

Likewise, “one meeting, 16 points” for Plum should carry almost no weight.

## 11. It does not score both sides

The universal and slip skills require bidirectional evaluation. 

Linemaker only presents Overs.

That introduces direction bias:

```text
search high recent overs
→ rank high recent overs
→ never test whether under or alternate line is stronger
```

## 12. It does not verify exact board identity

The output shows sportsbook lines, but not:

* retrieval timestamp;
* direct or aggregated source;
* exact event ID;
* exact settlement;
* whether the line remains available;
* whether the listed odds refer to the same market period.

Your exact-line auditor requires all of those fields before market comparison.

# What WOW should borrow

## Add a Prop Persistence Score

Use Linemaker’s multi-window idea as a discovery feature:

```text
persistence_score =
weighted agreement across
L5
L10
L15
L20
season
role-matched sample
```

But it must affect **research priority**, not final probability.

Suggested weights:

```text
role-matched current sample: 35%
season: 25%
L10: 15%
L20: 15%
L5: 10%
```

L5 should remain the smallest weight.

## Add a Threshold Cushion metric

For each prop:

```text
threshold_cushion =
median_projection - line
```

Also publish:

```text
mean cushion
median cushion
standard-deviation cushion
25th-percentile cushion
```

The 25th percentile is more useful for high-hit-probability screening than the mean.

## Add a Window Agreement flag

```text
FULL_ALIGNMENT
PARTIAL_ALIGNMENT
RECENT_ONLY
SEASON_ONLY
CONFLICTING_WINDOWS
```

A prop with 90% L10 but 56% season should not be treated like a prop with 75% across every window.

Kayla McBride is a perfect example:

```text
L10: 90%
season: 56.7%
```

That should trigger:

```text
RECENT_FORM_DIVERGENCE
OUTLIER_OR_ROLE_AUDIT_REQUIRED
```

## Add a Hit-Rate Inflation Audit

Require checks for:

* role change;
* schedule strength;
* opponent changes;
* outlier games;
* teammate absences;
* unsustainable shooting;
* small samples;
* stale season totals.

The June calibration audit already established the need for role-split ledgers and outlier isolation. 

## Add distribution-specific models

### MLB 0.5 hits

Model:

```text
projected plate appearances
hit probability per PA
starter split
bullpen split
batting-order position
pinch-hit/substitution risk
```

Then:

```text
P(1+ hits)
```

### WNBA points

Model:

```text
minutes
usage
shot attempts
free throws
three-point attempts
efficiency distribution
blowout/foul risk
```

### WNBA assists

Model:

```text
minutes
potential assists
teammate conversion
on-ball role
primary teammate availability
pace
turnover risk
```

### WNBA threes

Model:

```text
minutes
three-point attempts
shot quality
opponent allowance
conversion uncertainty
```

## Add Same-Player Opportunity Mutex

For Plum points and assists:

```text
same_player_same_event=true
shared_minutes_risk=true
shared_role_risk=true
```

Only one should normally survive unless joint dependence is explicitly modeled.

## Add “today only” hard filter

Before scanning:

```text
session_date
user_timezone
event_date
event_start
```

Any tomorrow row must go into a separate section:

```text
NEXT_DAY_PREVIEW
```

Never mix it into “remaining today.”

# Best new patch

```text
WOW-PATCH — MULTI-WINDOW PROP PERSISTENCE AND DISTRIBUTION AUDIT

1. Multi-window hit rates are discovery features only.
2. Require L5/L10/L15/L20/season and role-matched samples where available.
3. Classify window agreement.
4. Any L10-season gap >=20 percentage points triggers outlier/role audit.
5. Historical hit rate cannot be published as model probability.
6. Require a stat-specific distribution model.
7. Publish raw probability, calibrated probability, lower bound and interval.
8. Score both directions.
9. Tomorrow events cannot enter today's remaining slate.
10. Same-player and same-game candidates require duplicate-thesis and correlation review.
11. “Law of averages,” hot streaks and one-game matchup samples cannot support qualification.
12. Final ranking uses calibrated lower bound, not historical hit rate.
```

## Final conclusion

Linemaker is strongest at:

```text
wide discovery
multi-window hit-rate summaries
low-threshold identification
readable prop explanations
```

WOW should remain stronger at:

```text
correct slate
exact live line
role-matched samples
outlier detection
stat-specific distributions
dynamic calibration
bidirectional scoring
failure paths
same-player correlation
final refresh
```

The most valuable feature to borrow is **multi-window persistence scoring**. The most important thing not to borrow is its habit of treating historical hit rate as predictive probability.
