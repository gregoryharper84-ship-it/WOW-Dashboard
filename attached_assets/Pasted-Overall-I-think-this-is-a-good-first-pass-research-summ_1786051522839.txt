Those kinds of inconsistencies don't necessarily invalidate the recommendations, but they do reduce confidence in the supporting narrative.

### Probability vs. edge

The strongest part of the write-up is recognizing that:

* **High probability** asks, "How often does this team win?"
* **Edge** asks, "Is the market underestimating how often this team wins?"

Those are different questions.

For example:

* If New York's true win probability is 79% and you're paying 77¢, that's only a small edge.
* If Philadelphia's true win probability is 71% and you're paying 65¢, that's a much larger edge despite Philadelphia being less likely to win outright than New York.

That's the distinction your skill should continue to make.

### One recommendation I'd change

The Astros run line recommendation isn't necessarily justified by the information presented.

Moving from the moneyline to **-1.5** changes the distribution of outcomes substantially. You'd want evidence such as:

* historical margin-of-victory distribution,
* bullpen mismatch,
* late-inning run expectancy,
* lineup strength after the sixth inning,
* park effects.

A team can win frequently while failing to cover a run line often.

### How I'd improve your daily skill

One thing I think your engine should do that this analysis doesn't is explicitly separate every candidate into two independent leaderboards.

**Probability leaderboard**

* True win probability
* Lower confidence bound
* Failure-path score

**Edge leaderboard**

* Market no-vig probability
* Model probability
* Lower-bound edge
* Conservative EV
* Confidence grade

That prevents a common mistake: choosing the most likely winner instead of the best-priced wager.

I would also add a **confidence tier** for every recommendation:

* **Tier A** — High probability **and** verified positive edge.
* **Tier B** — High probability but fairly priced.
* **Tier C** — Positive edge but higher variance.
* **Tier D** — Narrative only; insufficient evidence.

That tiering system mirrors how professional betting models separate "good teams" from "good bets," and I think it would make your cross-sport skill substantially more robust.
