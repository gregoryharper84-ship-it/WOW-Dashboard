## Extracted mechanism

- Thesis **A**: one player; two exact props; joint success event `A = A1 ∩ A2`.
- Thesis **B**: second player in a separate game; `B = B1 ∩ B2`.
- Thesis **C**: third player in a third game; `C = C1 ∩ C2`.
- Tickets: **AB**, **AC**, **BC** (four legs each), and **ABC** (six legs).
- Staking example: `0.25 unit` on each ticket, total at risk `1.00 unit`.
- If exactly two theses succeed, one four-leg ticket pays and the other three lose. If all three succeed, all four tickets pay. If fewer than two succeed, all four tickets lose (ignoring push/void and correlated settlement specifics).

### Auditable payout identities

Let decimal gross-odds on the four tickets be `d_AB`, `d_AC`, `d_BC`, `d_ABC`, with initial stakes each `0.25 unit`:

- Realized **net return** (units): `R = 0.25[d_AB·1(AB) + d_AC·1(AC) + d_BC·1(BC) + d_ABC·1(ABC)] - 1`.
- If exactly A and B succeed, net is `0.25*d_AB - 1`. Breakeven requires `d_AB = 4.0`; profit requires `d_AB > 4.0`.
- The caption's asserted **+1.5 units profit when two players hit** implies the winning four-leg ticket has `d = 10.0` decimal gross odds (9:1) for this 0.25 unit stake, assuming no pushes, voids, fees, or stake adjustments. This price is not independently documented by an exact four-leg offer or settlement receipt and must not be generalized to every pair.
- Expected net units: `E[R] = 0.25[d_AB·P(A∩B) + d_AC·P(A∩C) + d_BC·P(B∩C) + d_ABC·P(A∩B∩C)] - 1`.
- The *joint probabilities* must be generated by fitted/certified governing models or certified dependence adapters. Multiplying marginal hit rates or treating all six legs as independent is not a valid substitute.
- Payout coverage changes **payoff shape**; it does not mathematically create an edge in otherwise negative-expectation selections.

## Valuable intelligence worth preserving

1. **Player-thesis abstraction**: Evaluate whether multiple props are manifestations of one certified player-role/game-script scenario. Track a single thesis key plus its constituent exact stat/line/direction legs. This may improve explanation and cross-slip exposure tracking.
2. **Ceiling/regime research**: Test alternate-line and multi-stat exceedance under fitted minutes, usage, pace, rotation, and matchup regimes rather than relying only on point forecasts or recent-hit-rate heuristics.
3. **Cross-game diversification hypothesis**: Three different games may reduce shared game-script exposure versus three players in one game, but does not remove league-wide injury/news/rotation or same-day market factors; quantify rather than assert independence.
4. **Coverage-package simulation**: Compare AB/AC/BC/ABC against independently qualified single tickets and other feasible structures on the *same pregame candidate universe, real prices, and total at-risk unit*.
5. **Profit tracking and audit discipline**: Record settled losses, exact captured payouts, timestamped offers, immutable pregame predictions, and all attempted/rejected cards. Self-reported $119K profit is not proof without verifiable logs, sample size, exposure, and fees.
6. **Risk-balanced fractional sizing as a subject of study**: Test fixed-fraction vs constant nominal units under a realistic return distribution and drawdowns. Constantly increasing stake size based on assumed wins may magnify ruin risk; do not adopt automatic unit escalation.

## Critical challenges / failure modes

- **Correlated is not inherently safer**: Pairing two props on one player can cause both to fail from the same early foul/injury/role shift. This **reduces distinct-player count but increases common-hinge concentration**.
- **One thesis ≠ one model event until quantified**: For example, points-over and assists-under may share a plausible narrative but could have complex conditional dependence. Rebounds-over and assists-over could jointly benefit from minutes yet also compete in certain game scripts.
- **Market payout reduction**: Same-game correlated prop payouts may be heavily adjusted. A six-leg headline 30:1 return tells little about the true fairness of each pair ticket.
- **Double counting portfolio exposure**: A, B, and C recur on three tickets each (A in AB, AC, ABC; etc.). One A failure can eliminate three tickets. Cross-ticket common-hinge exposure must be audited.
- **No guaranteed partial win profit**: Two successful player theses yield one winning ticket, but its **net** depends on actual gross odds, rules, and stake.
- **Unverified historical profit**: The video claim cannot establish long-run model skill, edge, or reproducibility absent transaction-level tracker and verified settlement/market prices.
- **Compounding narrative risk**: Projecting +10 units every month for 18 months is a scenario assumption, not a valid forecast; incorporate variance, drawdown, changing edge, and risk-of-ruin limits.

### The compounding arithmetic is reproducible, not an edge claim

If one unit always equals 1% of **current** bankroll and the hypothetical strategy earns exactly +10 units net monthly, monthly bankroll would grow by **10%**. From $10,000, the ordinary exact compound calculation produces:

| Active NBA months | Flat $100 unit: cumulative profit | 1% of current bankroll, +10 units/month: cumulative profit | Video's approximate scaled profit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 9 | $9,000 | $13,579.48 | $13,570 |

These figures corroborate the *mechanical compounding illustration*, with minor rounding differences. They **do not** corroborate that +10 units is achievable, predictable, calibrated, or a conservative return. Compounding negative monthly returns also magnifies the effect of drawdowns and leaves no guarantee of recovery.
